1. Volume alone won't get there

The target needs ~24% off per doubling of volume; a typical curve gives ~15%.

Agility writes that cost reduction is not mainly about volume, and that the biggest levers are engineering, supplier engagement and manufacturing experience. The math agrees.

24%cheaper with every doubling, to go from $75k at 1,000/yr to $30k at 10,000/yr
~$43.7kwhere a typical 15% curve would land at 10,000/yr
~$13.7kper robot left for design-to-cost and supplier work

Method: Wright's law, using production rate as a stand-in for cumulative volume (that's how the targets are stated). The 15% typical rate is my assumption; 10 to 20% is a common range.

2. Where the cost is decided

Suppliers, the change process, and two BOMs on one floor.

The flow as Agility describes it publicly. Highlighted steps are my hypotheses.

  1. Design and engineering changes
  2. Watch
    Suppliers and contract manufacturers
    Quotes at 1,000 robots a year, new tooling, and tariff or "Buy American" risk, which Agility names in its filing. Most of the $13.7k gap is won or lost here.
  3. Watch
    Change control
    A Digit 5 BOM that keeps moving from prototypes to early access to general availability. Each cost-down ECO has to land with the right stock, or it causes a line-stop or excess.
  4. Watch
    Kit release and material planning
    In 2027, Digit 4 still ships (GXO's new sites start on it) while Digit 5 ramps: two BOMs, Digit 4 parts phasing out one by one.
  5. RoboFab assembly
  6. Test and deployment

My 16 weeks

  1. Wk 1–2Learn the build and the BOM. A shift on the RoboFab line, then map one module from supplier quote to kit to station.
  2. Wk 3–6Own clear-to-build for that module. On hand, on order and short for Digit 4 and Digit 5, by build; a daily shortage list with recovery dates.
  3. Wk 7–11Work its cost-down. Should-cost against quotes at 1,000 a year, ECO timing, and the Digit 4 run-out plan, with the supply planners and global supply managers.
  4. Wk 12–16Run it and prove it. Track cost, shortages and excess against the plan as builds go, fix what breaks, then take on a second module.
